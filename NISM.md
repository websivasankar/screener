## 📚 TOPIC 1: ROLE OF RESEARCH ANALYST

- DP = Depository Participant (agent)

## 📚 TOPIC 5: PRIMARY VS SECONDARY MARKET

| Market | Purpose | Example | Issuer Involvement |
|--------|---------|---------|-------------------|
| **PRIMARY** | Raise fresh capital | IPO, FPO | YES (issuer sells) |

- **SECONDARY** = Already issued (second hand)

**FPO** = Follow-on Public Offering (additional issue after IPO)

## 📚 TOPIC 1: THE 3 VALUES (SUPER IMPORTANT!)

| Value | Meaning | Changes? | Used For |
|-------|---------|----------|----------|
| **FACE VALUE** | Par/Nominal value | Rarely (only splits) | Dividend calculation |
| **BOOK VALUE** | Net worth ÷ Shares | Yes (with profits) | P/B Ratio |
| **MARKET VALUE** | Trading price | Every second | Buying/Selling |

**EXAM TRAP #1:**
*"Company declares 20% dividend. Market price = ₹100, Face Value = ₹10. Dividend per share?"*
**Answer: ₹2** (20% of FV ₹10, NOT market price!)

---

## 📚 TOPIC 2: KEY RATIOS (CALCULATIONS!)

### **P/E RATIO (Most Important!)**
**Formula:** P/E = Market Price ÷ EPS

**Example:**
- Market Price = ₹200
- EPS = ₹20
- P/E = 200 ÷ 20 = 10

**Interpretation:**
- Low P/E (5-10) = Cheap/Undervalued
- High P/E (30+) = Expensive/Overvalued

### **MARKET CAPITALIZATION**
**Formula:** Market Cap = Market Price × Total Shares

**Example:**
- Market Price = ₹150
- Shares = 2 crore
- Market Cap = ₹300 crore

### **ENTERPRISE VALUE**
**Formula:** EV = Market Cap + Debt - Cash

**Example:**
- Market Cap = ₹10 lakh
- Debt = ₹3 lakh
- Cash = ₹4 lakh
- **EV = 10 + 3 - 4 = ₹9 lakh** ✅

---

## 📚 TOPIC 3: BOND BASICS

**3 ESSENTIAL COMPONENTS OF EVERY BOND:**
1. **Face Value** - Principal amount (e.g., ₹100)
2. **Coupon Rate** - Interest % (e.g., 8% p.a.)
3. **Maturity** - When repaid (e.g., 5 years)

**EXAM TRAP:**
*"When interest rates RISE, bond prices:"*
a) Rise
b) Fall ✅
c) Stay same

**WHY?** Inverse relationship! New bonds offer higher rates → Old bonds less attractive → Price falls!

---

## 📚 TOPIC 4: TYPES OF BONDS

### **1. ZERO-COUPON BOND**
- NO periodic interest
- Issued at discount (buy ₹80, get ₹100 at maturity)
- Example: T-Bills, Commercial Papers

### **2. CONVERTIBLE BOND**
- Can convert to equity shares
- Fully / Partly / Optionally convertible

### **3. FLOATING RATE BOND**
- Interest rate changes with market
- Protection against interest rate risk

### **4. CALLABLE BOND**
- Issuer can redeem before maturity
- Has embedded call option

## 📚 TOPIC 1: GDP (GUARANTEED QUESTION!)

**GDP = Gross Domestic Product**
= Total value of goods/services produced in a country

**IMPACT ON MARKETS:**
- GDP Growing (7%+) → Economy strong → Stocks UP ✅
- GDP Falling (recession) → Economy weak → Stocks DOWN ❌

**3 METHODS TO MEASURE GDP:**
1. Product Method (output)
2. Income Method (income generated)
3. Expenditure Method (spending)

**EXAM FACT:**
All three methods give SAME result!

## 📚 TOPIC 2: INFLATION (GUARANTEED!)

**INFLATION = General increase in prices**

**TWO MEASURES IN INDIA:**
1. **CPI** (Consumer Price Index) - Retail inflation
2. **WPI** (Wholesale Price Index) - Wholesale inflation

**GOOD INFLATION:** 2-4%
**BAD INFLATION:** 8-10%+

**EXAM QUESTION:**
*"Inflation is measured using which index?"*
**Answer: Both CPI and WPI** ✅

## 📚 TOPIC 3: FISCAL VS MONETARY (SUPER IMPORTANT!)

|--------|------------|-------|------------------|
| **FISCAL** | Government (Finance Ministry) | Taxes, Spending | Government budget |
| **MONETARY** | RBI (Central Bank) | Repo Rate, CRR, SLR | Money supply, Interest rates |

**MEMORY TRICK:**
- **FISCAL** = **FI**nance Ministry = Government Budget
- **MONETARY** = **MONEY** = RBI controls money

**EXAM TRAP:**
*"Monetary policy is managed by Government. True/False?"*
**Answer: FALSE** (RBI manages it!)

---

## 📚 TOPIC 4: RBI TOOLS (CALCULATIONS POSSIBLE!)

### **1. REPO RATE** (Most Important!)
= Rate at which banks borrow from RBI

**Impact:**
- Repo Rate ↑ → Banks borrow costly → Lending rates ↑ → Economy slows
- Repo Rate ↓ → Banks borrow cheap → Lending rates ↓ → Economy grows

### **2. CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio)**
= % of deposits banks must keep with RBI

**Impact:**
- CRR ↑ → Less money to lend → Money supply ↓
- CRR ↓ → More money to lend → Money supply ↑

### **3. SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio)**
= % of deposits banks must keep in liquid assets (G-Secs, Gold)

**Current values (approximate):**
- Repo Rate: ~6-7%
- CRR: ~4-5%
- SLR: ~18-20%

### **MICROECONOMICS:**

**EXAM TRAP:**

## 📚 TOPIC 1: PORTER'S FIVE FORCES (MOST IMPORTANT!)

**DEVELOPED BY:** Dr. Michael Porter (1979)

**THE 5 FORCES (All should be LOW/WEAK for attractive industry!):**

| Force | Good (Attractive) | Bad (Unattractive) |
|-------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1. New Entrants | LOW threat (high barriers) ✅ | HIGH threat (easy entry) ❌ |
| 2. Substitutes | FEW substitutes ✅ | MANY substitutes ❌ |
| 3. Rivalry | LOW competition ✅ | HIGH competition ❌ |
| 4. Supplier Power | WEAK suppliers ✅ | STRONG suppliers ❌ |
| 5. Buyer Power | WEAK buyers ✅ | STRONG buyers ❌ |

**EXAMPLE - AVIATION INDUSTRY:**
- New Entrants: Hard (govt approval) ✅
- Substitutes: YES (trains, buses) ❌
- Rivalry: VERY HIGH ❌
- Supplier Power: HIGH (only Boeing/Airbus) ❌
- Buyer Power: HIGH (price sensitive) ❌
**Result: 4/5 BAD = Unattractive industry!**

**EXAM TRAP:**
*"Which is NOT Porter's Force? Government Regulations"* ✅
(Government Regulations is in PESTLE, not Porter!)

## 📚 TOPIC 3: BCG MATRIX

**4 CATEGORIES (Based on Growth & Market Share):**

```
                HIGH Market Share | LOW Market Share
HIGH Growth  |   ⭐ STARS         |  ❓ QUESTION MARKS
LOW Growth   |   🐄 CASH COWS    |  🐕 DOGS
```

### **DEFINITIONS:**

**⭐ STARS:**
- High growth + High share
- Example: TCS in early 2000s
- Action: Invest more!

**🐄 CASH COWS:**
- Low growth + High share
- Example: Colgate toothpaste
- Action: Milk for cash!

**❓ QUESTION MARKS:**
- High growth + Low share
- Example: Tata Nano (failed), Pulsar (succeeded)
- Action: Risky investment!

**🐕 DOGS:**
- Low growth + Low share
- Example: Pager companies
- Action: Exit!

**EXAM GUARANTEED:**
*"Cash Cow = Low growth + High market share"* ✅

## 📚 BALANCE SHEET EQUATION

**Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders' Equity** ✅

**Always balanced!**

## 📚 KEY RATIOS (CALCULATIONS!)

### **CURRENT RATIO**
= Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities
- Good: 1.5 to 2
- Bad: Below 1

### **DEBT-TO-EQUITY**
= Total Debt ÷ Shareholders' Equity
- Good: Below 1
- Bad: Above 2

### **ROE**
= Net Profit ÷ Shareholders' Equity × 100
- Excellent: Above 20%
- Good: 15-20%
- Poor: Below 10%

## 📚 CASH FLOW SECTIONS

1. **OPERATING** - Business operations (MUST be positive!)
2. **INVESTING** - Buying/selling assets
3. **FINANCING** - Debt/equity activities

**CRITICAL:** Operating Cash Flow MUST be positive for healthy company!

## 📚 DCF (Discounted Cash Flow)

**Concept:** Company worth = Present value of future cash flows

**Warren Buffett:** "Intrinsic value is the discounted value of cash that can be taken out during remaining life"

**Don't need full calculation - understand concept!**

## 📚 P/E RATIO (MOST TESTED!)

= Market Price ÷ EPS

**Interpretation:**
- 5-10: Cheap
- 15-25: Fair
- 30+: Expensive

**MUST compare with industry average!**

## 📚 P/B RATIO

= Market Price ÷ Book Value per Share

- Below 1: Trading below book value
- 1-3: Reasonable
- Above 5: Expensive (unless high ROE!)

## 📚 DIVIDEND YIELD

= Annual Dividend ÷ Market Price

**Example:**
- Dividend = ₹20
- Price = ₹500
- Yield = 4%

## 📚 TWO TYPES OF RISK

### **SYSTEMATIC (Market Risk)**
- Affects entire market
- Cannot diversify away
- Examples: War, recession, inflation

### **UNSYSTEMATIC (Company Risk)**
- Affects specific company
- CAN diversify away
- Examples: Bad management, strike

## 📚 BETA

**β = Volatility vs Market**

- β = 1: Moves with market
- β > 1: More volatile than market
- β < 1: Less volatile than market
